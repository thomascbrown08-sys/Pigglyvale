    A Wing of the Kingdom

    The Dojo

    The practice yard, where the kingdom rehearses the conversations it is bad at — slowly, out loud, and with somebody laughing in the background.

    You have stepped into the practice yard. Somebody is being told to run it again. Old Ambrose is on the low wall with a jar of the dark honey, which is generally a sign.

    Nothing that happens here happened. The characters put on a mask, run a scene, and stop halfway through to argue about it. Bruno has never spoken to anybody the way he speaks in the green belt drills and would be appalled to be told he had. He is acting, badly, and enjoying it more than is decent.

    Each drill gives you a scene and one sentence that lands on you. You choose what you would say — and then you find out what it caused, three weeks later, with the consequence in it. Ambrose says what he thinks, and only afterwards does he put a name to what you did. The names are kept back on purpose: a labelled answer gets picked because it is labelled, and teaches nothing but a script.

    Come in by the sentence, not by the person

    The masks below are sorted by what was said to you. That is deliberate, and it is worth knowing why before you pick one.

    A mask is not a kind of person. It is a sentence somebody tells themselves so that what they are doing can go on feeling alright — which means it is a thing people put on and take off, and it means you have worn most of these yourself. Every case here has two halves, and the second half is always the mask on your own face.

    So: find the sentence you actually heard. Do not go looking for the one that sounds like somebody you know.

    How the belts work → — worth five minutes before you start, and necessary before the higher ones.

    The masks

    • The Helper “I was protecting you.” White belt
    • The Mind-Reader “I know how you really feel about me.” Green belt
    • The Turn “I can't believe you'd accuse me of that.” No face on it yet
    • The Peacemaker “I was being kind.” No face on it yet
    • The Softener “No, honestly, it's alright.” No face on it yet
    • The Rulebook “I'm only following the rule.” No face on it yet
    • The Expert “I'm the one who knows about this.” No face on it yet
    • The Joke “I was only teasing.” No face on it yet
    • The Ledger “After everything I've done for you.” No face on it yet
    • The Standard “I'm just honest. I have high standards.” No face on it yet

    Five of those are worn in the stories by people you like. That is not an accident and it is not a criticism of them. It is the whole argument for a practice yard: if masks only ever turned up on villains, nobody would need to learn them.
