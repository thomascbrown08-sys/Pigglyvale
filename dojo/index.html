    A Wing of the Kingdom

    The Dojo

    The practice yard, where the kingdom rehearses the conversations it is bad at — slowly, out loud, and with somebody laughing in the background.

    You have stepped into the practice yard. Somebody is being told to run it again. Old Ambrose is on the low wall with a jar of the dark honey, which is generally a sign.

    This is a yard, not an infirmary. You come here in the weeks when nothing is happening, so that you have already said the hard sentence forty times before the afternoon it matters. If you have arrived in the middle of something, you are welcome and it will still help — but the reason a yard is any use is that nothing here costs anybody anything.

    Nothing that happens here happened. Bruno has never spoken to anybody the way he speaks in the green belt drills and would be appalled to be told he had. He will put on whatever you need him to, badly, and enjoy it more than is decent, because that is what a sparring partner is for.

    Each drill gives you a scene and one sentence that lands on you. You choose what you would actually say — then you find out what it caused, three weeks later, with the consequence in it. Ambrose says what he thinks, and only afterwards does he put a name to what you did. The names are held back on purpose: a labelled answer gets picked because it is labelled, and teaches nothing but a script.

    Choose by your own weak side

    The list below is not a field guide to other people. Read it that way and you will go looking for the one that fits somebody you are cross with, and you will have learned nothing except a new thing to accuse them of.

    Read it the other way round. Which of these do you fold against? Which sentence, said to you on a Tuesday, would have you agreeing to something untrue by Tuesday evening — or saying something you would want back? That is the mask to train against, and it has almost nothing to do with who said it.

    You have worn most of them yourself, which is why every case has two halves and the second one is always the mask on your own face.

    How the belts work → — five minutes, and worth it before you start.

    • The Helper “I was protecting you.” Hard for you if — you cannot refuse a kindness without feeling ungrateful. White belt ready
    • The Mind-Reader “I know how you really feel about me.” Hard for you if — you feel you have to prove what you were really thinking. Green belt ready
    • The Turn “I can't believe you'd accuse me of that.” Hard for you if — you end up apologising and cannot reconstruct how. No face on it yet
    • The Peacemaker “I was being kind.” Hard for you if — you say yes to keep a face from falling. No face on it yet
    • The Softener “No, honestly, it's alright.” Hard for you if — you say it is fine when it is not, and resent it later. No face on it yet
    • The Rulebook “I'm only following the rule.” Hard for you if — you go quiet the moment somebody is technically correct. No face on it yet
    • The Expert “I'm the one who knows about this.” Hard for you if — you stop arguing because they know more than you do. No face on it yet
    • The Joke “I was only teasing.” Hard for you if — you laugh along and feel the sting an hour later. No face on it yet
    • The Ledger “After everything I've done for you.” Hard for you if — you pay debts you never agreed to. No face on it yet
    • The Standard “I'm just honest. I have high standards.” Hard for you if — you accept sharpness as the fair price of honesty. No face on it yet

    Five of these are worn in the stories by people you like. That is not an accident and it is not a criticism of them. It is the whole argument for a practice yard: if masks only ever turned up on villains, nobody would need to learn them.
