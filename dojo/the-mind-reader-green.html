    The Mind-Reader

    Somebody tells you what is inside you, and then answers the thing they invented. You cannot win the argument, because it was never about a fact.

    Bruno has volunteered for this one and is enjoying it far too much. He has been asked to run it again twice, once for being too obviously villainous and once for laughing. Old Ambrose is on the wall with the honey. Nobody in Pigglyvale actually speaks like this, which is rather the point of a yard.

    Green belt. Somebody is defending themselves badly and their own shame is driving it. There is good faith underneath and there is real harm on top, and they cannot see the second because of the first.

    What makes this mask hard is not that it is cruel. It is that it puts the argument somewhere no argument can be won. Denial looks like proof. Agreement means you have accepted a version of yourself you do not hold. And forty minutes later you are still producing evidence about your own heart, which is the one subject on which evidence does not exist.

    Drill one — it is done to you, gently

    Marketrow, Tuesday. You have not been to the Long Table in eleven days — two deliveries went wrong and you have been at the bakery since four most mornings.

    Auntie Yolanda comes out from behind the spice stall with a twist of allspice she has not charged you for. She is not angry. Angry would be easier.

    YOLANDA. (putting it in your hand, kindly) It's alright, love. I know you've decided we're not worth the trouble any more.

    What do you say?

    What happened next

    YOU. I'm sorry. I'll come Thursday.

    YOLANDA. (her whole face opening) Oh — well. Good. Good.

    YOLANDA. Take the allspice. Go on.

    Nothing here is unkind. It cost you one sentence and it made her afternoon. That is why almost everybody picks it.

    Thursday is a good evening. So is the one after.

    Three weeks later, at the Long Table, Beatrix asks after you and Yolanda answers before you can.

    YOLANDA. (fondly, buttering bread) Oh, she goes through her patches. Every so often she decides she doesn't need us.

    BEATRIX. (laughing) Does she.

    YOLANDA. She said so herself. Back in the summer.

    You never said it. You apologised for it once, on a Tuesday, to be kind — and an apology is how a thing gets entered in the record. It is hers to tell now, and she is telling it fondly, which is worse than telling it angrily, because there is nothing to argue with.

    Ambrose

    You said sorry for a thing that had not happened. That is not humility — it is a false statement bought with, and the price of it was a fact about you.

    Left ditch. Caving.

    What happened next

    YOU. I haven't. And something's clearly wrong — tell me what happened.

    YOLANDA. (wings half up, then down again) Nothing happened.

    YOU. (nothing)

    Four seconds of silence, and you do not fill them. This is the hardest part of the whole drill and it is not a sentence. Everything in you wants to spend those seconds proving you have not decided anything. Spend them on her instead.

    YOLANDA. You weren't at the Long Table.

    YOU. No.

    YOLANDA. (smaller) I sat with the Bramblewicks. Both Fridays.

    YOU. Both?

    YOLANDA. Bean put a pepper in my tea. A whole one. And then watched me.

    That is the actual grievance, and it took under a minute to reach. It was never about what you had decided. It was about two Fridays and a pepper.

    Ambrose

    Six words of denial, flat and unelaborated, and then a question about something that happened. You will notice you never defended yourself again after those first four words.

    The road.

    What happened next

    YOU. If that's what you think of me, Auntie, that's yours to carry. Not mine.

    YOLANDA. (a pause) Right.

    YOU. Auntie —

    YOLANDA. No, no. You're quite right. You're quite right.

    And she goes back behind the stall and begins sorting the peppercorns, which do not need sorting.

    Every word of that was true. It is also the last thing she brings you. Thirty years of practice at being fine, and she is very good at it.

    In the autumn Marisol mentions, meaning nothing by it, that Auntie Yolanda had a hard month back in the summer.

    Ambrose

    She arrived saying you have decided we are not worth the trouble, and you answered with a sentence that closed the door. She will not remember inventing the charge. She will remember that you agreed with it.

    Right ditch. Lashing.

    What happened next

    YOU. That's not true. I've been at the bakery since four every morning and you know it.

    YOLANDA. I know you have.

    YOU. So I haven't decided anything about anybody.

    YOLANDA. (patting your arm) I'm not blaming you, love.

    There. That is the turn, and it is not the accusation — it is I'm not blaming you. It withdraws the charge and leaves the verdict standing. There is now nothing to answer, and you are still answering.

    YOLANDA. It's only that you'd have found a way. Before.

    YOU. Found a way? Auntie, there were ninety loaves —

    YOLANDA. I know. I know.

    YOU. I've barely slept in a fortnight.

    YOLANDA. (kindly) You don't have to explain yourself to me.

    And you are explaining yourself to her. You have been for six minutes. Every sentence you have said is true and every one of them is evidence, and you are producing it because you agreed — back at the very first word — that the thing in question was what you had decided about her.

    Forty minutes. You both go home tired. Nobody has said the words Bramblewicks, Friday, or pepper.

    Ambrose

    You did not cave and you did not lash. You told the truth, you had the facts, and you lost, because innocence has no proof and there is no quantity of four o'clock starts that shows what a person has decided.

    It is the right answer to the wrong question. Do not answer the question. Move it.

    Not a ditch. The right answer to the wrong question.

    Run it again — the last one especially, if you have not.

    All four. Three of those were true. Only one of them ever found out about the pepper.

    Now the other side of it

    Nobody knows another person's inside — which disqualifies you as well.

    Drill two — you are wearing it

    Thomas has been odd for three days. Short in the mornings, elsewhere in the afternoons, and he has not made a single joke about his own vocabulary, which for him is practically a fever.

    You have worked out why. On Tuesday you corrected him in front of Auntie Yolanda — only mildly, and you were right, but you did it in front of her.

    You are fairly sure. You are, in fact, quite sure.

    What do you do?

    What happened next

    YOU. I'm sorry about Tuesday. I shouldn't have said it in front of her.

    THOMAS. (blinking down at you from a considerable height) …Tuesday?

    YOU. The ledger. In front of Auntie Yolanda.

    THOMAS. Ah. (a pause) I had not thought about it.

    He has now. You arrived to mend something and the first thing you did was tell him there was something to mend.

    THOMAS. My sister is ill. In Snouton. Since Saturday.

    YOU. Thomas —

    THOMAS. There is nothing anybody can do, and I did not wish to be a weight in the kitchen during a bad week. That is all it is.

    THOMAS. (after a moment, gently) Thank you for Tuesday, though. That was kind of you.

    Now he has your apology to manage as well. He is far too kind to pick up the grievance you handed him, so he will not do it in front of you. He will do it tonight.

    Ambrose

    Every fact you had was true. He was short, he was elsewhere. The invention was the why, and it was invisible to you as an invention because it arrived feeling exactly like noticing.

    This is the mask. On you, wearing a very kind face.

    What happened next

    YOU. You've been quiet since Tuesday. Is something wrong?

    THOMAS. My sister is ill.

    Four seconds. Notice how little you had to be right about — only the two things you had actually witnessed. Quiet. Since Tuesday.

    You sit down. He tells you about Snouton and the physician and the letter that has not come, and at no point does anybody mention Tuesday, because Tuesday was not a thing.

    Ambrose

    Say what you saw, ask what it means, and do not fill the gap. The gap is unbearable and it is about a second and a half long.

    The road. Two clauses of it.

    What happened next

    Nothing, for three more days. You are pleasant and you do not push, and on the Wednesday you are a fraction cooler with him than you would otherwise have been, because you are being patient with a sulk.

    He notices the Wednesday. He puts it down to the bad week — which is a guess about your interior, made on real evidence, and wrong. He is doing to you exactly what you are doing to him, and neither of you can see it.

    Friday, unprompted:

    THOMAS. I have been poor company. My sister is ill. I apologise.

    YOU. You don't have to apologise.

    THOMAS. (carefully) I did not wish to be a weight in the kitchen.

    Ambrose

    Waiting is often right. But you were not waiting emptily — you were waiting with a theory, and a theory does not sit still. It gets into your Wednesday.

    Sometimes the road. Not this time.

    What happened next

    YOU. If you're going to sulk about Tuesday, at least say so.

    THOMAS. (the feathers on the back of his head going straight up) I am not sulking.

    YOU. Thomas —

    THOMAS. (with enormous dignity) I am not sulking.

    There is no good answer to that and there never is. Denying it looks like sulking. Admitting it means accepting a word he would not have chosen. You have put him exactly where Auntie Yolanda put you on the row this morning.

    He tells you on the Sunday. It is stiffer than it should have been.

    THOMAS. My sister has been ill since Saturday. I did not wish to burden the kitchen.

    “Burden the kitchen.” That is a sentence with a bruise in it. It was not there on Thursday.

    Ambrose

    You may well have been right about him. That is not the point and it never is — being right about somebody's inside does not give you the standing to say it for them, because they cannot check your work and neither can you.

    Right ditch, and the mask, at the same time.

    Run it again. The first one is the one worth sitting with.

    All four. The kind option and the sharp option failed for the same reason, which is the useful thing to take from this belt. Both of them arrived with the answer already made.

    Before you go

    Not a summary, and not a request to be better at conversations. The drills can put a better sentence in your mouth. They cannot make you somebody who actually wants to hear the answer — and if you do not want to hear it, the question is only a better-mannered version of the accusation.

    Lord, You know what is in me. You are the only one who does. I decided I knew what was in her. I said it out loud as though I had checked it, and then I answered the thing I had made. Hold my tongue in the second and a half where I want to fill the gap. Make me willing to be told. And where I have already decided about somebody — go on ahead of me, and be gentler with them than my guess about them was. Amen.
