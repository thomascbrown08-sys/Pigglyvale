    The Long Winter

    The winter is true. Nobody is faking. And the oil still did not come — so how do you hold both?

    Bruno is wearing this one and has asked, unusually seriously, that everybody remember his mother is fine. Old Ambrose is on the wall with the honey.

    Green belt. They are defending themselves badly and it is their own exhaustion driving it. There is good faith underneath and real cost on top, and they cannot see the second because of the first.

    The hardship in every drill is true. Nothing is invented, nobody is faking, and there is no drill where it turns out to have been a story. If you leave here better at suspecting people, this page has failed and you should stop using it.

    Drill one — it arrives as the reason

    Bruno had the lantern oil for Thursday. It did not come. The Long Table ate in the half-dark and Gus went over a bench and has a shin the colour of a plum about it.

    Bruno's mother has been ill in Snouton for five weeks. He has been on the ridge road twice a week. Everybody knows.

    BRUNO. I know. I know. It's just — you know what this month's been like for me.

    What do you say?

    What happened next

    YOU. Of course. Don't give it another thought.

    BRUNO. (the relief coming off him like heat) You're a marvel, Majesty.

    He needed that and it was kind. It is also the whole conversation, and Thursday is still coming, and nothing has been said about Thursday.

    A fortnight later the oil does not come again. This time he does not raise his mother, because he does not need to — the exemption is already in place and both of you know it, and neither of you has ever said so out loud.

    He has been quietly moved out of the group of people who bring things. Nobody told him. He will notice in about four months, when something is being arranged and he is not asked.

    Ambrose

    You forgave a debt he had not asked you to forgive, and in doing it you answered a question he was not asking. He wanted to know he was still alright with you. You told him he was no longer counted on.

    Left ditch. Caving — the warm kind that looks like grace.

    What happened next

    YOU. I know it has. It's been a rotten month and I'm sorry about your mother.

    YOU. And the oil didn't come, and Gus went over a bench. What do we do about next Thursday?

    BRUNO. (a pause; he had not expected a second half) …I could leave it with Marisol on the Wednesday. She's there anyway.

    YOU. Do that.

    Both, in one breath, and neither cancelling the other. The winter is real and acknowledged in the first sentence. The bench is real and unmoved in the second. Notice that the question at the end is about Thursday, not about him — you did not ask him to account for himself, you asked him to solve a problem, which is a thing people can still do while they are drowning.

    The oil comes. It comes every Thursday after that, by way of Marisol, until his mother is better and then for a while longer out of habit.

    Ambrose

    You left him in the group of people who bring things. That is the kindest available act and it does not look like kindness at the time, which is why almost nobody does it.

    The road.

    What happened next

    YOU. Everyone's had a hard month, Bruno.

    BRUNO. (quietly) Right. Yes. Sorry.

    It is not true and he knows it is not true. Not everyone has been on the ridge road twice a week. You reached for a general fact in order to avoid a particular one, and the particular one was his mother.

    He brings the oil the following Thursday, early, and does not stay for the meal. He is careful with you for a long time after that, in the way people are careful around somebody who has shown them where the edge is.

    Ambrose

    You can hold somebody to a thing without levelling their winter to do it. Flattening it was not firmness — it was the shortest route to the same place, and it cost you something you will not get back quickly.

    Right ditch. Lashing.

    What happened next

    YOU. Shall I take the oil run off you for a while? Until things settle.

    BRUNO. (after a beat) …If you think that's best.

    Read that answer again. He did not say yes. He said if you think that's best, which is what people say when they have been told something about themselves and are too tired to argue with it.

    He does not ask for it back. Six weeks later his mother is better and the oil run is still Marisol's, and the reason it is still Marisol's is that nobody has raised it, and the reason nobody has raised it is that raising it would mean saying out loud that he was taken off it.

    Ambrose

    Generous, and it decided a thing that was his to decide. If you are going to offer this, offer it as a question with a real no available — do you want it off you for a month, or do you want to keep it? — and mean the second half.

    And notice which mask that was. You will find it two doors down.

    Not a ditch. The Helper, wearing this one's coat.

    Run it again. Three of these are things a kind person does.

    All four. Only one of them left him still counted on, and it was the one that mentioned the bench.

    Drill two — it arrives as the reason you should not have raised it

    One thing changes. In the first drill the winter explained a failure. Here it objects to the conversation itself.

    Bruno drew forty units of oil against the fair account in June. The fair account closed in May. It is not theft, it is not much, and it has to be squared before Miss Quill's quarter.

    You raise it on a Tuesday.

    BRUNO. You're bringing this up now? With everything I've got on?

    What do you say?

    What happened next

    It keeps. Miss Quill's quarter closes without it and the forty units sit in a column with a query against them.

    In October she raises it. Formally. In writing. To both of you.

    You did not spare him the conversation. You changed who would have it with him, and when, and how much paper would be involved. Tuesday in a kitchen was the gentlest version available and you gave it away.

    Ambrose

    Almost nothing gets dropped. It gets rescheduled, usually to a worse room, and generally to somebody less fond of him than you are.

    Left ditch. Caving.

    What happened next

    YOU. Let's do it next week, when things are calmer.

    BRUNO. (gratefully) Thanks, Majesty.

    Next week his mother is still ill. That is not a trick and he is not scheming — it is simply that winters do not have a calm week in them, and you have made calm the condition. There is no version of next week that arrives.

    You raise it three times over five weeks. Each time it is a worse moment than the Tuesday would have been, and each time you feel slightly more like a person who keeps bringing it up.

    Ambrose

    A deferral needs a date on it or it is a refusal in better clothes. Thursday, ten minutes, whatever else is happening is a deferral. When things are calmer is a wish.

    Not a ditch. A postponement with nowhere to land.

    What happened next

    YOU. There's never a good time with you, is there.

    BRUNO. (very still) My mother's dying, Majesty.

    She is not, as it turns out. But he thinks she might be, and he has been thinking it for five weeks on a ridge road at night, and nobody has asked him.

    You said a true thing about a pattern to a man who was not thinking about patterns. Even where the observation is fair — and it may be — it was aimed at a person rather than at forty units of oil, and it arrived as a verdict on his character in the middle of the worst month of his life.

    Ambrose

    Keep the subject the size it actually is. The subject was forty units. You made it him.

    Right ditch. Lashing — and it was accurate, which does not help.

    What happened next

    YOU. The timing's bad. I know it is.

    YOU. It's still the thing I have to talk about, and it's ten minutes.

    BRUNO. (a long breath) …Go on, then.

    It takes six. He signs the docket. Miss Quill's quarter closes clean.

    You conceded the timing and not the conversation, and those are two separate things that almost always get handed over together. He was right that it was a bad week. He was not right that it meant next month.

    On the way out he says, to the door rather than to you, that she is a bit better this week. Which is the first thing anybody has heard about her in a fortnight, and it came after the ten minutes rather than instead of them.

    Ambrose

    Notice when he told you about his mother. Not while it was buying something — afterwards, when it was only true. People will often hand you the real thing the moment it has stopped being a lever, and not one second before.

    The road.

    Run it again. The second is the one reasonable people choose.

    All four. Set this beside drill one. Same winter, same man — the only thing that moved is that this time the hardship was objecting to the conversation rather than explaining a failure, and three of the four answers ended the conversation.

    Now the other side of it

    Those were the two where it was worn at you. Here are the two where you have had the year, and it is true, and you have it in your hand.

    Drill three — you have a true reason and you are about to spend it

    You said you would write the letter supporting Beatrix's oven claim. You did not write it. The quarter closed on Friday and she has to wait until spring.

    It has been the worst month you can remember — the road, the money, Quill's rule, the business with Bruno. All of that is true and every soul in the kingdom knows it.

    She is in the kitchen. She has just found out.

    What do you say?

    What happened next

    YOU. I'm so sorry — it's been an impossible month, with the road and the money and —

    BEATRIX. (immediately) I know it has. Don't. Honestly.

    Every word of that was true and it functioned as a shield. She came in with a cracked oven and a lost quarter, and she left having reassured the Princess about the Princess's month. Watch how fast it happened — one sentence.

    She does not mention the oven again until March, and when she does she apologises for mentioning it.

    Ambrose

    You did not lie. You put a true thing in the doorway so that the other thing could not get through, and she was too kind to climb over it.

    The mask. On you, and made entirely of facts.

    What happened next

    YOU. I didn't write it. There's no excuse — and the month isn't one.

    YOU. What can I do now?

    BEATRIX. (flour to the elbows, thinking) You could write to Quill for the spring one now. It'd be first in.

    YOU. Tonight.

    Naming the excuse and refusing it in the same clause is what keeps this from being either grovelling or cold. And the question at the end moves the whole thing forward — she gets to say what would help, instead of being asked to absorb an apology.

    Ambrose

    You will notice it cost you the sympathy. It was available, it was deserved, and you did not take it. That is the entire drill.

    The road.

    What happened next

    YOU. I did say I might not get to it.

    BEATRIX. (after a moment) Did you?

    YOU. I think so.

    You did not say that. You thought it, in October, and by now the thinking and the saying have grown together, which is how this one always happens. It is not a lie exactly. It is a memory that has been quietly helpful to you.

    She says right, and goes back to the dough, and something small closes.

    Ambrose

    This is the one to be frightened of, because it does not feel like the others. The winter at least gets stated out loud where somebody could disagree with it. This one edits the record and nobody can check.

    Right ditch. Not lashing — the other thing that lives there.

    What happened next

    You write to Quill that night about the spring claim. It is first in. It is genuinely the most useful thing anybody could have done and it costs you an evening.

    Everything you did was right and one thing was missing. She never heard you say that you did not do it. So the version she has is that the quarter was lost to the general awfulness of the year — which is nearly true, and which quietly leaves her wondering whether she was somewhere near the bottom of a list.

    She never asks. That is the cost, and you will not be billed for it in any way you can see.

    Ambrose

    Doing the repair without saying the sentence spares you the four seconds and spares her nothing. The sentence is not a formality. It is the part that tells her the failure had an author.

    Not a ditch. Repair without confession.

    Run it again. The first one is the one that is hardest to catch, because it is all true.

    All four. Only one of them said the word I next to the word didn't.

    Drill four — you genuinely cannot, and you are entitled to say so

    The hardest one, because here the winter is not an excuse. It is a fact, and it is a fact that ought to change something.

    Thomas asks you to take the harvest accounts this week. You cannot. You are at the end of yourself and the month has been what it has been, and everybody knows that too.

    The question is not whether to say no. It is what the no is made of.

    What do you say?

    What happened next

    YOU. Thomas. You know what this month has been.

    THOMAS. (spectacles sliding) Ah. Yes. Of course. Forgive me, Majesty.

    He apologised for asking. That was not the plan and it is what happened, because a hardship offered instead of an answer turns the request itself into an offence — and now he is carrying something he did not do wrong.

    He stops asking you things for a fortnight. Two of them were things you would have wanted to be asked.

    Ambrose

    The month is real and it was still not the answer to his question. He asked whether you would take the accounts. No is an answer. The month is a reason, and reasons handed over instead of answers ask the other person to do the deciding and then feel bad about it.

    The mask. On you, and completely deserved, which changes nothing.

    What happened next

    YOU. No. Not this week.

    THOMAS. Right. (a pause) Right.

    And he goes away and works out something else, which takes him two days and involves Gus.

    Clean, honest, and no sympathy spent — which is most of what this drill is for. It is a genuinely good answer.

    It left him with the whole problem and no information. He did not know whether this week meant next week, or whether somebody else would do, or whether he had asked the wrong person entirely. He spent two days finding out what one sentence would have told him.

    Ambrose

    A no needs nothing attached to be legitimate. It often needs something attached to be useful, and those are different requirements. Do not confuse being entitled to say a thing with having said it well.

    Not a ditch. The road, with the help left off.

    What happened next

    YOU. I can't take that this week.

    YOU. I could do it the week after. Or Quill could do it better than I would, if you asked her.

    THOMAS. (brightening) I shall ask her.

    The winter does not appear anywhere in that. It did not need to. The no stands on I can't, which requires no evidence and invites no sympathy, and the second sentence hands him something to do with it.

    Miss Quill does the accounts and finds an error in the spring figures that nobody else would have found.

    Ambrose

    You spent nothing and you gave him a route. And notice what you kept: the month is still yours to tell him about later, on a night when it is only true, and it will mean something because you did not use it here.

    The road.

    What happened next

    You take them. You do them at eleven at night for four nights. They are late and there are two mistakes in them and everyone is very kind about it.

    This is the mask worn without saying a word, and it is the meanest version of it, because it cannot be answered. Nobody can decline a claim that was never made out loud. Thomas now owes you something he never agreed to owe.

    He does not ask you for anything for a month. Neither does anybody else. You have taught the whole kitchen that asking you costs them.

    Ambrose

    Visible suffering in place of a sentence is the loudest thing a person can do and the hardest to have a conversation about. If you want the credit, say the no. If you want the no, say the no. Do the accounts badly on purpose and you have chosen neither.

    The mask. In silence, which is where it is strongest.

    Run it again. The first one is deserved, which is exactly the difficulty.

    All four. The winter was real in every one of them. It was load-bearing in two, and in the other two it did not need to be mentioned at all — which is how you can tell the no was standing on its own feet.

    Before you go

    Not a summary, and not a request to be better at conversations. This mask is made of true things, so no amount of honesty will dislodge it. What it needs is to stop being necessary.

    Lord, You did not argue with the man at the pool. Thirty-eight years, and You did not say they were fewer than he remembered. I have used what is true to get out of what is mine. I have put a real weight in a doorway so that a small thing could not get through, and I have called it honesty. You asked him whether he wanted to be well. Ask me. And when I answer with the reasons I cannot, ask me again. Carry what I actually cannot carry. Give me back the ordinary things — the oil on a Thursday, the letter I said I would write — because being counted on is not a burden You took from me. It is one of the ways You keep me. Amen.

    Now read what is under the Long Winter → — the man at Bethesda, and why the road never disputes the years.

    Practise it

    1. Say drill one's answer out loud, both halves. “I know it has — and the oil didn't come.” Notice how badly the word and wants to be but. It is not but. Both are standing.
    2. Say drill three's first clause: “There's no excuse, and the month isn't one.” That is you naming your own best defence and setting it down, which is a thing you can only do about yourself.
    3. The harder one. Think of the last true, heavy thing you said that ended a conversation. What was the conversation about? Is it still unfinished?
