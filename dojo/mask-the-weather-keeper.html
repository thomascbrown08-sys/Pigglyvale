    The Weather-Keeper

    “I should have known how that would land.” — the belief that other people's insides are yours to manage, and that if you were only careful enough, nobody would be unhappy.

    “I should have known how that would land.”

    Train it if it is worn at you somebody's mood arrives in the room and becomes your job without anybody asking. If a heavy silence sends you hunting for what you did.

    Train it if you reach for it you read the room and adjust yourself before anybody has requested anything — and you cannot sit down while a person nearby is unhappy, whatever the cause of it.

    This is the mask of keeping the weather. It is the only one on the shelf whose wearer is usually doing more harm to themselves than to anybody else — which is exactly why it goes unnoticed for decades, and why people are praised for it.

    It sits at white belt because in the ordinary case nothing was aimed at you at all. Somebody is tired, or grieving, or cross about a delivery, and you took it as a summons. Beatrix wears it in the first drill without meaning a single thing by it.
