## 2. Real gaps, ranked

### 1. Repair — how an apology is actually made *(new chapter, Part IV)*
**The largest gap, and the one Pigglyvale most needs.**

The book covers what happens *inside* a person who has done wrong (**Ch. 21**, guilt that repairs vs shame
that consumes) and what happens on the *receiving* end (**Ch. 22**, forgiveness, reconciliation and trust as
three separate things). Between those two sits the act itself, and it has no chapter.

What it would carry: naming the thing without the passive voice; no excuse attached in the same breath;
saying what you will do differently; and then the hardest part, which is stopping — not asking to be told it
is alright. That last one is where most sincere apologies fail, and it is the whole of the Weather-Keeper's
yellow belt.

It also has an obvious biblical spine that the book is already equipped for: Zacchaeus naming a figure, and
Matthew 5:23–24 putting the altar second.

*Sits naturally after Ch. 14, before Ch. 15.*

### 2. Anger *(new chapter)*
There is no chapter on it. For a book that takes on the problems DBT was built for — *"emotions that arrive
at the wrong size"* — that is a conspicuous absence, and it leaves half of Pigglyvale's geometry with
nowhere to point. The Dojo's two ditches are caving and lashing; caving has **Ch. 14** and **Ch. 15** to
walk to, and lashing has nothing.

The material is there in the tradition and it is not simple: anger as a reading rather than noise
(**Ch. 13** already establishes the frame), *be angry and do not sin*, and the honest problem that
righteous anger is the easiest thing in the world to counterfeit from the inside.

### 3. Church hurt *(new chapter, Part VI)*
Part VI has the adjacent material — **Ch. 19** care that was not care, **Ch. 23** a name they did not give
you, **Ch. 24** the record that survives — but nothing that names harm done *by a church, in God's name*,
which is the case where the injury and the place you would go for help are the same building. My Toolbox has
**Institutional betrayal**; the Christian counterpart is missing here, and this is the book that should
carry it.

Part VI's existing guards make it the right home rather than a risk.

### 4. Verify contingent worth is actually covered
**Ch. 8** and **Ch. 16** may already do it. What to check for specifically: the split between *am I useful
here* and *am I wanted here* — two questions that sound identical from outside, where a ledger genuinely
answers the first and nothing answers the second, so the sufferer keeps producing better answers to a
question nobody asked. If that distinction is not explicit somewhere, it needs a home, because it is the
engine under Miss Quill and under the whole Keep chart.

## 3. Structural, and cheap

- **The two sites do not know about each other.** Keep Your Heart and My Toolbox are companion resources
  with no cross-links. A reader landing on **Ch. 21** should be able to reach *Toxic shame vs. guilt*, and
  the reverse. One editing pass over the chapter feet and the pattern pages.
- **Chapter feet have no *where to go next*.** The Lament Finder proves the model works — *what you are
  feeling → which psalm already said it.* A two-line pointer at the end of each chapter would close the
  loop with no new writing.
- **The Judgment-Call Log is an unusually good idea** and worth saying so. If any of the additions above get
  written, they belong in it.

## 4. What Pigglyvale needs from this site

doors now, and they are deliberately different:
