    The Long Winter

    “You know what kind of year I've had.” — and they did. That is what makes this one difficult, and it is why the drills never once let the hardship be false.

    “You know what kind of year I've had.”

    Train it if it is worn at you somebody's real hardship arrives whenever accountability does, and you cannot work out how to hold both — so you drop the second one.

    Train it if you reach for it you have spent a true, terrible year to get out of a small thing you did not want to do, and you did not notice you were spending it.

    Read this before the drills, because it is the mask most able to do damage in the wrong hands.

    In every case here the hardship is real. Bruno's mother genuinely is ill. The year genuinely was terrible. Nothing is invented and nobody is faking, and if you go looking for the drill where somebody turns out to have made it up, there isn't one — because a reader who learns to suspect suffering has learned the wrong thing entirely and will use it on the next person who tells her the truth.

    The mask is not the wound. The mask is what the wound gets asked to do.
