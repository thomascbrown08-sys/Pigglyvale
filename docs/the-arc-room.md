# 1. Episode 002 — *The Column Nobody Read*

> **Written before the register change. Now current.** Story 1,800–2,200. Page order is story →
> After the Washing-Up → Advisor's Notes (ending on the Movement card) → Talk It Over. All analysis prose
> follows `voice-for-carolyn.md`. Reference sheets for Quill and Marisol must be rolled and approved
> **before** the episode art — see `prompts/00-reference-sheets.md`, Round Five.

## The spine

**Primary lesson:** boundaries — trust is earned by evidence, and promoting on charm costs the person who
already earned it.

**Background lesson:** Biblical principle reasoned out.

**Harm engines:** Carolyn's own overshoot, *good intention / bad execution* — a rotation, since Thomas
carried that engine in 001. Then Miss Quill's *rule over person*, firing as a **consequence** of the injury
rather than as a first cause. Two engines chained is new for the series and worth doing.

**Who apologizes:** Carolyn to Quill, on the page, three parts. Then **Quill to Carolyn** for the ledger
retaliation — a character who has never apologized, doing it badly and meaning it.

**Thomas is not due.** He apologized in 001; the rule is every three or four. So 002 is where he gets to be
*wrong without carrying the repair* — a different and rarer job. **Old Ambrose corrects him inside the
Notes** on his four-minute promotion of Marisol; the bible requires this at least once and it has not
happened yet. Then 003 opens with Thomas owning it, which discharges the other half of the rule.

## The cast move

**Marisol Vega** — a pig. Travelling baker with a portable clay oven, arrives as Fair Week ends. Genuinely
warm, genuinely funny, genuinely excellent, and genuinely useful, because **Beatrix's big oven cracked in
Episode One and Gus said three weeks.** Her value is real. That is what makes the overshoot understandable
rather than foolish.

She is not a con artist and she never lies. Her only failing is that she accepts everything she is offered,
which is not a crime.

## The eight beats

1. **Marisol arrives** and is immediately, visibly good. Bread in a cracked-oven week. Marketrow loves her.
   Thomas loves her within four minutes and says so at length — *"she is one of us"* — his canonical flaw
   operating in plain sight and unremarked.
2. **Carolyn hands over the kitchen.** Not the keys to a building; the keys to herself. Marisol is at the
   worktable within days.
3. **The unfinished thing.** Late, tired, flour everywhere, Carolyn says something true and exhausted —
   *"Some days I would give anything for one person here who didn't need me to be delighted about their
   columns."* It is not cruel. It is the **unfinished version of herself**, and it is the one key nobody
   should hand to somebody they met on Tuesday.
4. **Marketrow.** Marisol repeats it in perfect good faith, warmly, as a way of showing she belongs. It gets
   back to Quill inside an afternoon.
5. **Quill goes by the book.** Her engine fires. Every informal dispensation Carolyn has enjoyed for twenty
   years is suddenly, correctly, denied. Nothing she does is wrong. Everything she does costs.
6. **Carolyn flares** — the sharp turn the bible warns about, arriving three weeks late and landing on the
   wrong person. Then she sees it.
7. **The repair.** *"I gave a stranger something that belonged to you, and then I let you find out at
   Marketrow."* No excuse attached. And Quill's answer is not about the ledger at all — **"Twenty years, and
   nobody has ever asked me to sit down."** *(Butterflies here, and only here.)*
8. **The Long Table.** Carolyn does not over-correct. **One honest step**, and then the question nobody has
   asked in twenty years: *what is the column for?*

## The column

An established running gag — Miss Quill's ledger has a column nobody else understands — and this is where it
pays. **She records who has not been to the Long Table lately.** Twenty years of noticing who is missing,
which is love in ledger form, from a woman whom order once saved when nothing else did.

Do not explain it. Let Carolyn read it and go quiet.

## After the Washing-Up — draft, and the Second Question is load-bearing

The Second Question is **Carolyn catching Thomas**, which rotates correctly: on the Keep page it was Thomas
asking her.

> **THOMAS.** I have been trying to put a name to what Delphine does.
>
> **CAROLYN.** *(not looking up from the pot)* Try it on me.
>
> **THOMAS.** She keeps those books the way a tenant pays rent. Every month, on time, in full, never once
> late — because she has got it into her head that the rent is what holds the roof up over her. *(his wing
> tip splayed into its rough point)*
>
> **CAROLYN.** That's close.
>
> **THOMAS.** *(the feathers on the back of his head going up)* Close.
>
> **CAROLYN.** It's the right picture, turned the wrong way round. She isn't paying rent so she can stay in
> the room. She thinks the rent is the only thing she has that anybody wants. Take the ledger off her and
> she couldn't tell you one reason a person would keep her.
>
> **THOMAS.** *(a long pause; his spectacles slid down his bill and stayed there)* …That is worse.
>
> **CAROLYN.** It's worse.
>
> **THOMAS.** And the carefulness that got her through—
>
> **CAROLYN.** —is the same carefulness people call cold now. She can't see it either. From the inside, a
> wall doesn't feel like a wall. It feels like doing the job properly.
>
> **THOMAS.** Then make it right. Put her at the Inner Court. Invite her to everything for a month and let
> her see.
>
> **CAROLYN.** And then?
>
> **THOMAS.** And then she is invited.
>
> **CAROLYN.** And then she knows why.
>
> **THOMAS.** *(a pause)* Ah.
>
> **CAROLYN.** If I hand her a seat because I feel badly, I have told her that a seat is the thing you get
> when somebody feels badly. Which is what she already believes. I'd be proving her right and calling it an
> apology.
>
> **THOMAS.** …Then what does work?
>
> **CAROLYN.** Asking her for something. A gift says *I owe you.* A question says *I need you.* Only one of
> those puts her anywhere.
>
> **THOMAS.** There is an old teaching about this, and everyone files it under heaven. A house you did not
> build. A room made up before you ever got there. Nothing you carry in through the door adds to it, and
> nothing you leave behind takes it away.
>
> **CAROLYN.** I know the one.
>
> **THOMAS.** I have begun to think it is also about kitchens.
>
> **CAROLYN.** You could put that in the book.
>
> **THOMAS.** I could put it in the book.
>
> **CAROLYN.** You could also just ask her what the column is for.

**Why the Second Question matters here and is not decoration.** It changes the ending. Thomas's first
answer — promote her, invite her to everything — is generous, obvious, and would deepen the wound, because a
seat handed over out of guilt confirms the exact belief that is hurting her. The corrected answer is to ask
her for something, and *that* is what the last beat of the story then does. The story's ending depends on
this exchange. Cut it and the ending is unmotivated.

**Sidelong line at the foot:** *The people who study this have a name for it — it's here, at My Toolbox, if
you ever want it.* The term stays in the link. It does not go in the dialogue.

## Notes for the writing session

- **Carolyn gets to be right at the end.** The invitation is the correct move, correctly sized, and she
  makes it without being told. Hard rule satisfied.
- **Marisol stays likeable and stays.** She is not run out of the kingdom. She ends at Marketrow, warmly,
  and does not know she was ever anywhere else. That is the honest outcome and it is kinder than exile.
- **Derivation for the Notes: Luke 16:10.** *"One who is faithful in a very little is also faithful in
  much."* Quill's whole case is twenty years of very little. The reasoning to show: faithfulness in small
  things is not a lesser kind of proof, it is the **only** kind that piles up — which is exactly why charm
  cannot stand in for it, since charm produces no small things at all.
- **Recipe card:** Marisol's pan sobao, or something else out of the portable oven. Name the dish; get it
  right.

# 2. The Keep as a recurring device

## The short answer on length

The skill says roughly 2,500 words, Notes 450–600. **2,000 is fine, and the reason to do it is not
concision.** It is that an episode should carry one move, not a syllabus.

Right now the story has to dramatise *and* explain. If the Keep does the explaining, the story is freed to
just happen. Three organs, one job each:

| Organ | Job |
|---|---|
| The story | It happens. No teaching, no summary, no moral. |
| The Movement card | It shows the mechanism, in one picture, specific to this episode. |
| Advisor's Notes | It reasons — one principle, one derivation, one thing to try. |

**The one rule for cutting to 2,000: cut setup, never cut repair.** The repair beat is the point of the
series and it is the first thing that gets squeezed. Drop a subplot rather than compressing every scene by
twenty percent.

## Why the full chart must not appear every episode

The butterfly rule is the precedent, and it applies here word for word: *spending them cheaply is the only
way to ruin them.* A six-ring chart in every episode becomes a logo. Worse, it turns the fiction into an
illustrated lecture, and the reader starts skipping to the diagram.

## The Movement card — the component I'd build instead

Not the whole map. **One person, one move.** Small, and different every time, so it can never become
wallpaper.

Three elements only:

- **Where they stood** — a dot on one ring.
- **Where they ended** — a dot on another.
- **The path between** — and this is the part that does the teaching.

Two path types, and the contrast between them *is* the lesson the reader asked for:

> **The clean move.** A solid line, one ring at a time, numbered with the rung that was used.
>
> **The skip.** A dashed arc leaping three rings inward, with no rung numbers on it — because none were
> used — and then a hard snap back to the outer edge. That single picture is the arithmetic paragraph
> from the Keep page, drawn. It is what "boundaries create chaos when skipped" actually looks like.

For 002, one card carries both: **Marisol** — Road ⇢ Long Table, dashed, no rungs, snap back to Marketrow.
**Quill** — Great Hall → Inner Court, solid, one step, earned.

**It lives in the Advisor's Notes, never in the story.** The story stays story. Thomas is the one holding
the book; the diagram is his.

**Say the word and I'll build the component** — a small inline SVG with a data block per episode, so writing
a card is filling in four values, not drawing anything.

# 3. The arcs

## Arc A — Broken promises, and forgiving someone you cannot trust

*The Watch, dramatised. This is the one the whole chart was built for.*

**Who:** **Bruno "Buckets" Marrow.** Already canon, already *shortsighted self-interest*, already never
malicious and never looking past next Tuesday. A pattern of broken promises without malice is precisely
him, and using him means no new character has to be trusted with this material.

**The trap to avoid, and it is a big one:** do not let Bruno repent at the end. If he repents and is
restored inside one episode, the episode teaches that the Watch is a short punishment with a happy ending,
which is the opposite of true and will not survive contact with anyone's actual life.

**So: Bruno does not change.** Carolyn forgives him — really, not as a technique — keeps him at Marketrow,
keeps buying from his cart, keeps being warm, and does not lend him anything. The episode resolves **in her**
and stays unresolved **in the relationship.** That is rare in children's fiction and it is exactly what was
asked for.

**Where the teaching lands:** not in her head. The kingdom notices. Gus grumbles that she is letting him off.
Somebody else says she is being hard. Carolyn has to say the difference **out loud, to other people** —
which is much harder than deciding it privately, and much more useful to watch.

**The sentence the episode exists to deliver:** *"I'm not angry with him any more. I'm also not lending him
the cart. Those aren't in a fight with each other."*

**Derivation: Romans 12:18.** *"If possible, so far as it depends on you, live peaceably with all."* Two
qualifiers sit in that sentence and a sentimental reading erases both. *If possible* concedes that sometimes
it is not. *So far as it depends on you* concedes that peace has a second party who may not cooperate. Paul
is describing exactly this situation, and he does not treat it as failure.

> **Theological guardrail, non-negotiable:** forgiveness stays **unilateral and immediate**. Nothing in this
> episode may imply that Carolyn withholds forgiveness pending Bruno's repentance. It is *trust* that is
> conditional, and *reconciliation* that needs two. Get that wrong and the episode teaches grudge-keeping
> with better vocabulary.

## Arc B — Offers too good to be true

**Do not make it a con.** Most bad deals are not lies, and an episode about spotting a liar teaches a skill
almost nobody needs. Make the offer **completely genuine** and let the cost live in the second order.

**The offer:** a trader will buy the entire Pepper Patch harvest, every year, at three times what Marketrow
pays. It is real. The money is real. Nobody lies.

**What it actually buys** is not peppers. It is a key. The shared ground goes to one crop, the informal
trading that holds Marketrow together stops, Bruno's cart has nothing to carry, and the kingdom's food
depends on one buyer who is under no obligation next year. The Pepper Patch is already flagged in the bible
as load-bearing for the economics track — this is what it was being saved for.

**Primary:** second-order thinking. **Background:** boundaries. **Engine:** shortsighted self-interest,
distributed — *everybody* wants the money, so there is no one to blame, which is the honest shape of it.

**The two portable lines:**

- **The price was never the price.** What is being bought is access.
- **Urgency is the tell.** An offer that requires a fast yes is telling you something about itself. Not that
  it is a lie — that it cannot survive being thought about.

## Arc C — Saying the expectation out loud

*The four kinds — hope, request, agreement, boundary — dramatised.*

**Who:** **Auntie Yolanda Plum.** Good intention, bad execution, listens poorly, thirty years of friendship,
and she will reorganise your kitchen because she loves you. Give the two of them a shared project.

**The engine of the episode is comic and then painful:** halfway through, each of them describes what they
agreed, and the two accounts are completely different. Neither is lying. Neither ever said it.

**The fix is the running gag paying off:** they write it down. **Miss Quill's ledger becomes the hero** —
which rhymes with 002 and starts building Quill toward the Inner Court across a season rather than in one
jump.

**Derivation: Luke 14:28** — the man who builds a tower and sits down first to count the cost. The reasoning
to show: counting the cost *out loud, before starting* is presented by Christ as ordinary prudence, not
pessimism. Saying the hard thing early is not doubt about the project. It is respect for it.

## Arc D — The ward

*The delicate one. Read this whole section before deciding anything.*

### Why it belongs to this series and not to some other one

The wound described is: **her belonging was used as a consequence.** When adults were frustrated with her
conduct, they moved her *standing*.

That is the exact inverse of the Watch. In a healthy Keep, standing is fixed and keys move. In that
childhood, the keys stayed and the **seat** was what got taken away.

Which means the series is already built for this. *Blood gets you the seat, conduct keeps the keys* is not a
line about family — it is a repair for that specific deformation, written before we knew what it was for.
And it explains the flaw with total coherence: if standing can be revoked, you buy it by saying yes to
everything, and you pre-empt the revocation by revoking first.

So the arc is not an emotional set-piece imported from outside. It is the series' own central mechanic seen
from the other side, and it will feel earned rather than staged.

### Four concerns, in order of seriousness

**1. "Become the parent you needed" can bypass the grief instead of touching it.** Giving a child what you
did not get is genuinely healing and it is also an excellent hiding place. If the resolution is *and now she
is whole because she gave it away*, the episode teaches substitution.

*The fix:* Carolyn must be on the **receiving** end too. Thomas or Ambrose says the sentence to *her*, in
the same episode. She does not only give the promise. She gets one.

**2. A child who fears displacement is a very sharp mirror.** A reader who identifies with Carolyn may find
herself identifying with the child instead, without warning. That is the ambush risk and it is real.

*The fix:* show the fear **lightly and briefly** — one or two small behaviours, never a catalogue. And ship
it after a run of episodes where Carolyn is competent and winning, not during a thin stretch.

**3. Do not let the ward be a device.** If the child exists only to heal Carolyn, it will read that way. The
ward needs wants of their own that have nothing to do with the theme — a stubborn opinion about a dog, a
feud with a Bramblewick, something entirely their own.

**4. Never explain it.** No Advisor's Note says what this is about. Butterfly logic: the pointing is the
point.

### The recommendation: build the ground first, obliquely

Do not open with the ward. Open with **Pim**, inside a larger episode, in about two hundred words.

Pim says yes to absolutely everything. People do that when they believe their place is conditional — which
makes her the mirror the bible already says she is. So: **Pim breaks something and expects to be dismissed.**
Not dramatically. She just starts, quietly, gathering her things.

And Carolyn says the sentence that becomes kingdom law:

> *"You don't lose your seat here. You might lose the spoon for a week."*

Small, funny, complete in one scene, and it establishes the rule at low stakes. Then whenever the full ward
arc arrives, it is not a new idea being introduced under pressure. It is Pigglyvale's own law, applied at
full weight to someone who has never heard it.

**And one more thing, which is not a craft note.** Before the ward episode is written, she should know it is
coming. A surprise is the wrong shape for this particular gift.

# 4. The case study — the test case

## What is available

Two existing bodies of work, both usable:

**Keep Your Heart** — sixteen chapters on emotional health founded on Scripture, DBT treated as a serious
interlocutor. Portable machinery: the **six-tier warrant system** (labelling how hard a passage is working),
the **Lament Finder** (emotional state → psalm), **nutshells** (a built tension compressed into one
memorable line), two-track Teaching/Practice registers, composite case studies.

**My Toolbox** — thirty-three pages, three categories: **Patterns** (internal mechanisms — double bind,
learned helplessness, parentification, moral injury, complex-trauma triad, disorganised attachment),
**Dynamics** (things done to a person), **Modalities** (CBT, EMDR, ACT, somatic work, Adaptive Disclosure,
and confession and spiritual direction as a modality in its own right). Every page closes with a *look up*
section. Governing method: a secular tool that works is naming a real human need, and that need has a more
faithful biblical counterpart.

## The concern that shapes the whole design

**Analyse the episode, or a secondary character. Never analyse Carolyn.**

A case study of a character modelled on the reader, sitting inside a story she reads for pleasure, is a
different object than a case study of a character she is watching. The first can feel like being written
about. Keep the clinical lens off the protagonist permanently — not just when it seems risky.

This is not a limitation. The secondary characters are better material anyway.

## The 002 test case: Miss Quill

**Pattern: contingent worth** — *I am allowed to be here because I am useful and exact.*

That is Quill in one line, it is already canon (order once saved her when nothing else did), and it explains
everything about her: why she keeps the books, why she never asked to be invited, and why being passed over
for a newcomer was not a slight but a confirmation.

**Roughly 250–300 words**, structured the way a Toolbox page is structured:

1. **Name the pattern** without jargon, in a sentence a reader recognises before they can define it.
2. **Show it in the episode** — one specific beat. Quill going strictly by the book is not spite. It is a
   woman returning to the only thing that has ever reliably held her.
3. **Say what it costs** — the adaptation that saved her at twenty is what gets read as coldness at forty,
   and she cannot see the difference from inside it.
4. **Look up.** Her place was never contingent. It never was for anyone.

That last move is worth noticing, because it is not decoration. **The Keep is already a theological
diagram.** *Blood gets you the seat, conduct keeps the keys* is a picture of standing given rather than
earned, with conduct governing privilege instead of belonging. The chart was drawn for boundaries and it
turns out to be carrying justification and sanctification.

> **Hold that analogy with a stated limit.** It illuminates and it does not map cleanly at every point —
> God does not withdraw His love as a key. Use it as an analogy that is *said to be* an analogy, not as a
> doctrine of the Christian life. Label the reach, the way the warrant system already labels it elsewhere.

**This episode also motivates a Toolbox page.** Contingent worth is currently on the backlog there. Writing
the case study first and the page second is a good order — the story shows you what the page needs to say.

## How to place it on the page

Between the Notes and Talk It Over, as asked — with one modification.

**Style it as a citation, not a new section.** Give it the Toolbox palette (cream, soft blue, gold) against
Pigglyvale's plum and mango, headed *From My Toolbox*, with a link to the fuller page. That way it reads as
a deliberate change of room rather than a tonal wobble, and Thomas's voice is not asked to stretch into a
register it does not have.

**Link one direction only** — Pigglyvale points at the Toolbox; the Toolbox never points back. Two sites
that cite each other have to be maintained together forever.
