    The Helper

    Somebody did a hard thing for you, kindly, without asking. Nothing was aimed at you. Something was taken anyway.

    The practice yard, after the tables are cleared. Thomas has agreed to wear this one on the grounds that it is his and he ought to know it best. Old Ambrose is sitting on the low wall with a jar of the dark honey, which is generally a sign. Bruno has been told he may not laugh. Bruno is laughing.

    Two drills. Pick whatever you would actually say — not the one you think is being asked for — and see what it caused. Then run it again and try the others, because the point of a yard is that you get to find out without it costing anybody anything.

    Drill one — it is done to you, and you find out after

    Gus Thornapple is three weeks behind on the stall levy and you are the one who has to say so. You have been working up to it since Tuesday, badly. You rehearsed the first sentence four times and the fourth was worse than the first.

    You come up Marketrow with it sitting in your chest, and it is done. Thomas has already spoken to him. Kindly, and well. The levy is settled by Friday and Gus was not even sour about it.

    THOMAS. (pleased, and pleased for you) Don't thank me. I could see you were dreading it.

    What do you say?

    What happened next

    YOU. Oh — thank you. That was kind of you.

    THOMAS. (crest feathers settling) Not at all, Majesty. Not at all.

    It was kind, and saying so cost you nothing — which is exactly the trouble. A true sentence that costs nothing is often the one doing the most work.

    Nine days later there is a thing with Beatrix's apprentice and he handles it before you arrive. A fortnight after that it is Auntie Yolanda and the lamp oil. Each time he is quicker off the mark, because each time he was thanked.

    GUS. (to Bruno at midsummer, not knowing you are behind him) When she's cross with you, she sends the duck.

    Two things left the row with that thank-you. You told him it worked. And you agreed to his account of you — that you were dreading it and needed carrying — which is not his any more. It is yours as well.

    Ambrose

    You have not begun a hard sentence in two months and nobody expects you to. That was not taken from you. It was handed over, once, on a Tuesday, in a pleasant tone.

    Left ditch. Caving.

    What happened next

    YOU. I didn't ask you to do that.

    THOMAS. (the feathers on the back of his head going straight up; his spectacles staying exactly where they are) I see.

    Watch the spectacles. When he is undefended they slide down his bill. They have not moved. He is not taking this in — he is bracing against it.

    YOU. Thomas —

    THOMAS. No, no. Quite right. I overstepped.

    He is scrupulously polite for four days.

    He does not stop stepping in. He stops mentioning it. The next time he settles something on your behalf you find out a month later, and you find out from Bruno.

    Ambrose

    Every word was true, and it aimed at him rather than at the thing he did, so the next ten minutes were about your tone. A sharp no does not change what people do — only whether they tell you.

    Right ditch. Lashing.

    What happened next

    You go up the row after the stalls have thinned out.

    YOU. Gus. The levy.

    GUS. (genuinely bewildered) The duck already came.

    YOU. I know. I'm saying it too.

    It takes forty seconds. It is smaller than it was in your chest, the way these things almost always are, and you walk home having actually done it.

    You got back the thing that was taken. Not the errand — the evidence. You found out you could, which is precisely what the help had cost you, and it is not something anybody can tell you.

    Three weeks later Thomas does it again. Nothing that happened told him not to. And Gus, who owed one levy, has now been spoken to about it twice.

    Ambrose

    Doing the brave thing privately, where the person who needs to hear it cannot, is a way of being brave without being clear. You will be standing here again in three weeks.

    Not a ditch. A half-measure — the road walked alone.

    What happened next

    YOU. That was kind, and I'd rather you hadn't.

    THOMAS. (spectacles sliding down his bill; he does not push them back up) Ah.

    YOU. I was working up to it. I needed to be the one who said it. Will you ask me first next time?

    THOMAS. (after a moment in which a great deal happens behind the spectacles) I did not ask. I did not ask because I was fairly sure of the answer, and I did not want it.

    Three things in one breath and none of them optional. The kindness named as real, because it was. The cost named without a verdict on him. And a request at the end — the part people leave off. There is a version of him that gets this right and you have just described it. He cannot aim at a door you have not shown him.

    Eleven days later Beatrix's apprentice does something foolish with a delivery.

    THOMAS. (in the doorway, with the appalling formality of a duck doing a new thing badly) Your Majesty. Do you wish to be asked about this?

    YOU. Yes.

    It is the least graceful sentence he has ever produced, and it is him trying.

    Ambrose

    You went to him, and you went to him alone, before it was anybody else's business. Our Lord puts that first for a reason (Matthew 18:15) — not as a procedure, but because a thing said between two people can still be mended by two.

    The road.

    Run it again — the others are worth seeing, and nobody is watching.

    All four. The two ditches look like opposites and are not. One buys you out of the awkwardness, the other out of feeling small. Both are about you.

    Drill two — it is done to you, in front of everybody

    Same mask, different afternoon. The first one you found out about after it was over. This one happens while you are sitting there.

    The Long Table, Friday. Gus asks down the length of it whether the Pepper Patch is staying common ground or being parcelled out, because he would like to know what he is digging in the spring.

    You have been turning it over all week. You have not decided. You open your mouth.

    THOMAS. (comfortably) Her Majesty and I discussed it. The patch stays common ground.

    You did discuss it. He is not lying. And it is now decided, out loud, in front of forty people, and the table has already nodded and moved on to the bread.

    What do you do?

    What happened next

    The table moves on. Nobody notices anything, because nothing visible happened.

    Sunday, in the kitchen:

    THOMAS. (spectacles sliding) I did that badly. I am sorry, Majesty. It will not happen again.

    A real apology, properly made — and it cannot reach the thing that actually happened, because the thing that actually happened was said to forty people on Friday and has been working on them ever since.

    Monday, Gus starts digging on the assumption the patch is common ground. By the time you have decided otherwise there are two rows of onions in it. You let the onions stand, because it was easier than explaining.

    Ambrose

    Some things mend afterwards and some have already left the room. A decision announced at a table of forty is the second kind. Sunday was the right conversation at the wrong hour.

    Left ditch. The polite version of caving.

    What happened next

    YOU. Thomas — I'd like to answer that one.

    THOMAS. (spectacles sliding) Of course, Majesty.

    YOU. Gus, I haven't decided. I'll say on Monday. What do you think it should be?

    GUS. (so surprised at being asked that he has to go and stand behind a wall about it)

    Eleven seconds, and all of it public, which is where the thing happened. You did not contradict him and you did not correct him. You took the floor back — which was always yours — and he handed it over without a wound, because you asked for the thing rather than objecting to him.

    And notice the cost. It is not the awkwardness. It is that you then had to stand there with an unfinished decision in front of the kingdom. He stepped in to spare you exactly that, and taking the floor back means taking the exposure with it.

    Ambrose

    There is no version of this where you get the standing and he keeps the discomfort.

    The road.

    What happened next

    YOU. Actually, I hadn't decided that.

    The table goes quiet in the particular way a table does when two people at the head of it are not agreeing.

    THOMAS. (perfectly gracious) Ah — forgive me.

    True, necessary, said at the right moment, and aimed a hand's breadth wrong. I hadn't decided that corrects him. I'd like to answer that one claims the floor. They achieve the same thing in the room and leave very different men standing there.

    For the next month he checks with you before saying anything at all, including things that plainly do not need checking, in a way that is scrupulously correct and slightly cold. Twice you catch yourself wishing he would just answer.

    Ambrose

    The difference costs you nothing, which is why it is worth the half-second it takes to choose.

    Right of the road. Barely, and it still cost a month.

    What happened next

    YOU. That's Thomas's view. I'll tell you mine on Monday, Gus.

    GUS. Right you are.

    Brisk, efficient, and the table never even pauses.

    You said that's Thomas's view in front of forty people. That puts a small distance between you on a matter he had thought you were agreed on — and he had some reason to think it, because you did discuss it.

    He does not mention it. He is quieter on the walk back than he was on the walk down.

    Ambrose

    You got the decision back, and you separated yourself from him publicly to do it, which is a larger coin than the situation required. He spoke out of turn. He did not turn out to be a stranger.

    Not a ditch. The road, taken at somebody else's expense.

    Run it again. The difference between the second and third answers is the whole of this drill.

    All four. Set this beside drill one. There you had all evening to choose your sentence and the cost was awkwardness. Here you had four seconds, and the cost was standing in public with an unfinished decision. Same mask — and the thing that got harder was not the wording.

    Now the other side of it

    Those were the easy half, and they are the half everybody wants. Here are the two that matter.

    Drill three — you are wearing it, and you can see her struggling

    Pim has to tell Gus Thornapple that his egg delivery was two short, and that it was two short last week as well.

    She has had her apron on since after lunch. She is by the kitchen door. She has been by the kitchen door for eleven minutes, and twice she has got as far as the step.

    You are at the worktable. You can see all of it.

    What do you do?

    What happened next

    YOU. I'll go, Pim. Don't worry about it.

    PIM. (her whole body loosening) Thank you, Majesty. Thank you.

    She thanked you twice. It is the second one that ought to bother you.

    You go. It takes four minutes. Gus is fine about it.

    In the autumn there is a thing with a delivery of flour, and Pim comes and finds you before she has been to the door at all. She does not stand there for eleven minutes any more. She has learned the shorter route.

    Ambrose

    Nothing unkind happened anywhere in that. It was quick, she was grateful — and you have taught her that the way through a hard conversation is to find somebody larger. She was two steps from finding out she could do it.

    This is the mask. On you, and it fits beautifully.

    What happened next

    YOU. Come on. We'll go together.

    PIM. (enormous gratitude) Yes, Majesty.

    And then, at the stall, one of two things happens — and which one has nothing to do with what you intended.

    GUS. (turning, and looking at you, because you are the Princess and you are standing there) Majesty?

    That is the fork, and it lasts about a second. Either you say nothing at all and she gets it out badly with one restart — or you answer him, which is the most natural thing in the world, and then it was your conversation and she was present at it.

    Ambrose

    Going with somebody is not going instead of them, and company is a real gift. But settle it on the walk down and say it out loud: I'll come, and I'm not going to say anything. Then she has both.

    Not a ditch, and not yet the road. It depends entirely on the next four minutes.

    What happened next

    She stands there a while longer. Then she goes.

    She comes back twenty minutes later having done it, and does not mention it, and you do not mention it either, and she is a fraction taller for a week.

    There was a version of that afternoon where she stood at the door until it got dark and then quietly took her apron off, and you would not have known, because you were chopping.

    Ambrose

    It worked, partly because you were right about her and partly because you were lucky, and from where you were standing those look identical. If you are going to be quiet, be quiet and available — where she can see you, not busy enough to be unreachable.

    Sometimes the road. Sometimes just not looking.

    What happened next

    YOU. Pim — do you want me to come, or would you rather do it on your own?

    PIM. (immediately, then looking faintly appalled at herself for having said it so fast) On my own.

    YOU. Right.

    You go back to the board. She is gone twenty-five minutes.

    PIM. He said it was the geese. I said it was still two short either way.

    Listen to how she reports it. She has already told herself the story twice on the walk home. That is the thing she got, and you could not have given it to her.

    You do not make anything of it. You ask her to check the onions.

    Ambrose

    You handed her the decision along with the offer, which is the difference between help and management. And it cost you something: you had to be willing to hear yes, come with me and mean it. An offer you are hoping will be refused is not an offer.

    The road. One sentence long, and not easy.

    Run it again. This one has no clean answers in it, which is the point.

    All four. Only one was certainly wrong, and it was the kind one. The mask is not cruelty wearing a smile. It is love with a fault in it.

    Drill four — you are wearing it, and nobody is struggling yet

    Drill three was a rescue. This one is a prediction, which is much harder to catch, because nothing has gone wrong yet and you are only being sensible.

    Bruno is putting the lantern-oil contract to Miss Quill on Thursday. You know exactly how it will go. He will arrive without figures and be charming about it; she will ask for the figures; he will improvise; and she will thank him for his time in under four minutes.

    It is Tuesday. You have not been asked to do anything.

    What do you do?

    What happened next

    Thursday goes well. Miss Quill is patient in a way she is not usually patient, asks for the figures twice instead of once, and he gets there in the end.

    BRUNO. (afterwards, delighted, and telling it for a fortnight) You've just got to know how to read her.

    He now believes something untrue about himself, on your evidence. He will act on it.

    In the spring he takes the same approach to the bread contract with Beatrix, who has had no quiet word from anybody, and it goes the way Thursday would have gone. He cannot work out why — and the information he needs is the one thing you cannot give him now without taking Thursday away from him retrospectively.

    Ambrose

    Nobody was deceived and nothing untrue was said. You put a thumb on a scale that was not yours, invisibly. This is the mask at its most comfortable — it never has to be defended, because nobody ever finds out.

    The mask. The invisible fitting.

    What happened next

    Four minutes. He comes out looking like a man who has walked into a door.

    On the Monday he asks Pim what a ledger column is for. In June he puts the same contract up with three years of figures on two sheets of paper, and Miss Quill approves it in eleven minutes and remarks, to nobody, that it is a decent piece of work.

    You had nothing to do with any of that, which is the point of it.

    Ambrose

    Sometimes the road is a closed mouth. But be careful how you hold it — doing nothing is the same shape as not caring, and only you know which one you did. If you stayed out because it was his to walk, that is respect. If you stayed out because you rather wanted to see it, that is a different afternoon wearing the same face.

    Often the road. Check your reason for it.

    What happened next

    YOU. Bruno — a word about Thursday. She'll want three years of figures, and she'll want them on paper.

    BRUNO. (taking it well) Right you are, Majesty.

    Thursday goes well and he is generous about where the help came from.

    Better than the first answer, and I want that said. It was open, he knew it was happening, and he kept his own Thursday.

    The next time he has something to put to anybody, he comes to you first — not for figures, but to find out whether you think it will land. By the autumn he is checking his own ideas with you before he is sure he has them.

    Ambrose

    What it skipped was two seconds long. You decided he needed it, and he did not get to decide — and nothing about it looked like a decision to him either. It looked like you being useful, which is how a habit gets made.

    Not a ditch. The road with the asking left out.

    What happened next

    YOU. Bruno — do you want to know how she reads a proposal, or would you rather go in cold?

    BRUNO. (immediately, with enormous confidence) Cold.

    Thursday takes four minutes. He comes out looking like a man who has walked into a door.

    On the Friday he finds you — not sheepishly, which is the interesting part, but like somebody placing an order.

    BRUNO. Right. Go on, then. How does she read a proposal?

    One sentence did three things. It offered. It handed him the decision. And it told him, without a word of warning in it, that there was something to know — which is why he came back on Friday instead of deciding she was unreasonable.

    Ambrose

    It cost you Thursday. You had to watch it having offered, which is harder than watching it having said nothing. That is the price of the asking: you have to be willing to hear cold and mean it.

    The road. Both halves — the offer, and letting him refuse.

    Run it again. The first and third are the two most people would call kind.

    All four. Set this beside drill three. There she was standing at the door with her apron on and you could see it. Here nothing had happened yet and you were only being sensible — and the mask fits better in this one, because there is no moment where it looks like rescue.

    Before you go

    Not a summary, and not a request to be better at conversations. The drills can put a better sentence in your mouth. They cannot make you somebody who is willing to stand still while a person you love does a hard thing badly — and that is the whole of what this mask needs.

    Lord, You got under the weight that would have crushed us, and You did not take from us the things we were made to carry. I stepped in without asking. I called it love, and some of it was love, and some of it was that watching is hard for me and I wanted it to stop. Give me the patience to stay in my chair. Loosen my grip on the outcome, which was never mine to hold. And where I have already taken a thing out of somebody's hands — show me, and give me the words to put it back. Amen.
