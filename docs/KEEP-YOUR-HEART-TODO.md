## ✎ FOR YOU TO THINK ABOUT — the Church Hurt dials

Deferred by request. These are the decisions that have to be made *before* drafting, because each
one changes what the chapter is. Bring answers, or bring the ones you can't decide and we'll work
them out.

**1 · Scope.** Four different things get called church hurt: a congregation that was cold or clumsy;
a leader who was domineering; systematic spiritual abuse with coercion and shunning; and cover-up of
criminal harm. Does one chapter carry all four, or does it name a range and pick one?

**2 · Stay or leave.** The hardest dial. Three options: never advise leaving; advise it under stated
conditions; or refuse the question and give the reader criteria to decide for themselves. There is
no neutral option — **a chapter that is silent on this will be read as counselling endurance**, and
for some readers that would be the wrong advice and a costly one.

**3 · Institution vs. Bride.** How hard does the chapter distinguish *the church that failed you*
from *the Church*? Ezekiel 34 gives a real move — God prosecutes the shepherds and does not abolish
the office. Pressed too far the same move becomes "don't blame the church, blame individuals,"
which is a dodge and readers will smell it. Where is the line?

**4 · The reader's own part.** Ch 23 and Ch 25 both refuse to let the reader off. Does this chapter
apply the same discipline? If yes, it risks landing on someone who was genuinely only sinned
against. If no, it breaks the book's most consistent pattern. My instinct is a bounded version —
say plainly that some readers have a part and some have none, and let the reader sort themselves —
but that is a real call and it is yours.

**5 · Naming.** Your standing rule is *address the teaching, never the tribe*. Some of these
patterns cluster in particular ecclesiologies — authoritarian eldership, high-control movements.
Does the rule hold here, or does honesty require naming structures rather than denominations?

**6 · God vs. his people.** When the harm was done in God's name, the reader's picture of God is
contaminated by it. How much of the chapter is disentangling those two? Arguably this *is* the
chapter and everything else is context.

**7 · Restoration.** Does the chapter hold out returning to a church as the hoped-for end? For some
readers that is the right hope. For others it is pressure applied to an injury. Where does it land?

**8 · Register.** Ch 21 is measured — deliberately. Church hurt could run hotter. Should it? A
colder chapter is more credible to a skeptical reader; a warmer one is more use to a wounded one.

**9 · Where it stops.** If criminal harm is in scope, the chapter acquires obligations. It will need
an explicit hand-off, like the crisis boundaries elsewhere.

**One structural note:** writing this chapter effectively decides the framing of the spiritual-abuse
page you are still holding in My Toolbox. Ch 21 was deliberately kept general so as not to preempt
it. Those two should be decided together.
