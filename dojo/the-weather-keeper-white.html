    The Weather-Keeper

    Nothing was aimed at you. You took it as a summons anyway — and then went looking for more.

    Beatrix has agreed to wear this one in the first drill, though she maintains she has never banged a tray in her life. Old Ambrose is on the wall with the honey. Bruno is here for the second one and has already been told to stop enjoying it.

    Four drills. Two where somebody's weather arrives and becomes your job, and two where you pick it up unprompted. The second pair are the ones that matter.

    Drill one — the weather has nothing to do with you

    Five in the morning. Beatrix is in the Kitchen Royal for the fair-bread and she is not saying much. She sets a tray down harder than a tray needs to be set down. She has not looked at you since she came in.

    Nothing has been said. Nothing has been asked. There is simply a great deal of weather in the room and you have nine hours of your own ahead of you.

    What do you do?

    What happened next

    YOU. Beatrix — have I done something?

    BEATRIX. (genuinely baffled) What? No.

    YOU. Only you seem —

    BEATRIX. The oven man's not coming till the ninth.

    It was never about you. It was never going to be about you. But the question has now put you in the room's weather as a possible cause, and she has to reassure you about a thing she was not thinking about, on four hours' sleep, before dawn.

    BEATRIX. (after a moment, more gently) It's not you, love. Honestly.

    And there it is. She is now looking after you. That is the actual cost of this one, and it is small and it happens every time.

    Ambrose

    You went looking for your own name in somebody else's bad morning, and when you could not find it, she had to help you look. Kindly meant. Still a tax.

    The mask. The first move it always makes.

    What happened next

    You make the coffee. You are lighter than you feel. You talk about the plantain order and Bruno's latest cart name, and by seven the room is easier and Beatrix is almost laughing.

    It worked. That is not a trick ending — it genuinely worked, and you are good at it, and this is why the mask stays on for thirty years.

    And at four in the afternoon you are flat on your back with nothing left, and you cannot account for it, because you did not do anything difficult today.

    You lifted a mood for two hours. That is work. It is simply work nobody can see, including you, so it never gets entered anywhere — which is how a person ends up exhausted and unable to say from what.

    Ambrose

    Nobody asked you to carry the morning and nobody will thank you for it, because nobody saw it happen. That is not a complaint about them. It is a fact about invisible work.

    The mask, in its most competent form.

    What happened next

    YOU. You're banging trays. Do you want to talk, or would you rather be left alone?

    BEATRIX. (a pause, hands still in the dough) Left alone. For an hour.

    YOU. Right.

    Two things happened in eleven words. You named a behaviour you actually witnessed — not a mood you inferred — and you handed her the choice instead of making it. She is allowed to be in a bad mood in your kitchen, and now she knows it.

    At about half past six she tells you about the ninth, unprompted, while scoring loaves.

    Ambrose

    You will notice you did not have to be right about anything. Trays are audible. And you gave up the outcome in the second half of the sentence, which is the part that cost you.

    The road.

    What happened next

    You get on with the peppers. She gets on with the bread. The room is not comfortable and nobody dies of it.

    At about half past six she says, to the loaves rather than to you, that the oven man is not coming till the ninth. You say that is a poor show. She agrees. That is the whole conversation.

    Often this is simply right, and a yard that only ever taught sentences would never say so. Not every silence is a summons and most weather passes.

    Ambrose

    Check one thing, and only you can. Were you leaving her alone, or were you avoiding her? They look identical from where I am sitting and entirely different from where you are.

    Usually the road. Check your reason.

    Run it again. The first two are the ones most people would call caring.

    All four. Nothing in that drill was aimed at you. That is the whole of white belt, and it is where nine-tenths of this mask lives.

    Drill two — the weather is doing the asking

    Same mask, one thing changed: this time the mood arrives just after you have said no.

    Bruno wants the cart at the Long Table on Friday. You have said no — there is no room, and you have said it kindly and for a good reason.

    BRUNO. (bright, immediate) Course. Course. No bother at all.

    And then he is subdued for the rest of the evening. He does not say anything. He does not sulk, exactly. He is just… smaller. Two people ask him if he is alright.

    What do you do?

    What happened next

    YOU. Oh — look, bring the cart. We'll find room.

    BRUNO. (instantly restored) Majesty! You're a marvel.

    Friday is cramped. Two benches get moved and Gus has to eat standing up.

    Nothing was said and the no was reversed. Which means the thing that reversed it was the mood — and Bruno now knows, without ever having been told and without having done anything cynical, that going quiet works.

    In the spring he wants the cart inside the Kitchen Royal.

    Ambrose

    You did not decide to move. You were moved, by weather, and you called it flexibility. The test is simple and it is not about the cart: would you have changed your mind if he had been cheerful about it?

    Left ditch. Caving — the version with no argument in it.

    What happened next

    YOU. It's only the room, Bruno. If we had the space I'd say yes tomorrow.

    BRUNO. I know. It's fine.

    YOU. It's not that anybody objects to the cart —

    BRUNO. Majesty. Honestly. It's fine.

    You are now on your third explanation of a decision nobody has disputed. Notice what you are actually asking him for. Not understanding — he understood at the first go. You are asking him to stop being disappointed, which he cannot do to order, and which is not his to hand you.

    By the fourth explanation he is comforting you.

    Ambrose

    A reason given once is information. Given three times it is a request for absolution, and the person you are asking for it is the person you just disappointed.

    Not a ditch. The right answer, said until it turned into a plea.

    What happened next

    YOU. Bruno, you've gone quiet. Is it the cart, or is it something else?

    BRUNO. (a beat too long) …It's the cart.

    YOU. Right.

    BRUNO. (hopefully) Is it — is there any way —

    You asked a question you already knew the answer to. That is not curiosity. From where he was sitting it was an offer to reopen, because why else would you raise it? And now you have to say no to him twice, which is harder than saying it once and is entirely your own doing.

    Everywhere else in this yard, asking is the road. Here it is the mask, and the difference is one thing only: whether you already know why.

    Ambrose

    Ask when you do not know. When you do know, and you ask anyway, you are not seeking information — you are trying to get the mood out of the room, which is the mask doing its work through a very reasonable-sounding sentence.

    The mask, disguised as the road.

    What happened next

    You do not mention it. You pass him the greens and ask what the cart is called this week, and he tells you, and it is a bad name, and you say so.

    He is quiet for another twenty minutes. Then he is not.

    Both halves are necessary and people usually do one. The no stands — you did not manage his feelings. And you stayed warm — you did not punish him for having them. Cold-and-firm is a different thing entirely and he would have felt it.

    Nothing else happens. That is the whole outcome. He is fine by the bread.

    Ambrose

    He is allowed to be disappointed. That is not a problem to be solved and it is not an accusation. It is simply what happens inside a person who wanted something and did not get it, and it passes on its own if nobody feeds it.

    The road. It looks like doing nothing and it is not.

    Run it again — the third one especially, since it is the one that reads as kind.

    All four. Set this beside drill one. There, asking was the road. Here, the same question was the mask. What changed was not the sentence — it was whether you already knew the answer.

    Now the other side of it

    Those were the half where somebody else's weather came to you. Here are the two where you go out to meet it.

    Drill three — you pick up a mood that is not yours

    The Long Table, Friday. Two people at the far end are not speaking to each other. It is about a fence. It has nothing to do with you, nobody has mentioned it to you, and neither of them has asked for anything.

    You can feel it from where you are sitting.

    What do you do?

    What happened next

    It works, because it always works. By pudding they are both laughing at something Bruno said, and the fence has not been mentioned once.

    You have eaten four mouthfuls of your own dinner and spoken to nobody for longer than a minute.

    The fence is still there. You did not fix anything; you postponed it, and you paid for the postponement out of your own evening. They will do this again in a fortnight, and so will you.

    Ambrose

    Nobody at that table asked you to hold it up. You are very good at this, which is the trouble — being good at a thing is the commonest reason people go on doing it long after anyone needed them to.

    The mask. Sixty souls, one hostess, no dinner.

    What happened next

    YOU. (quietly, by the lanterns) Is everything alright with you and Gus?

    THEM. (startled) — Yes? Why, what's he said?

    He has said nothing. But you have now told them that their fence is visible from the head of the table, and that the Princess has noticed, and a private disagreement has become a matter somebody else is tracking.

    They are careful in front of you for a month.

    Ambrose

    Kindly meant, and it made the thing larger. A quarrel two people can still mend between them is a quarrel that has not yet been observed out loud.

    The mask, with better manners.

    What happened next

    Thomas does it, because Thomas always does it, and he does it in the way you would expect — which is to say he tells each of them separately what the other one meant.

    You did not put the mask down. You handed it to a duck who was already wearing one of his own. Now there are two people managing a fence that belongs to two other people.

    Ambrose

    Delegating a thing that was not yours does not make it his. It makes it nobody's, and it is still not theirs, which is where it started and where it needed to stay.

    The mask, subcontracted.

    What happened next

    You eat your dinner. It is very good; you made it.

    The far end of the table stays cool all evening. Nobody else appears to notice, or if they do, nobody minds. At some point they leave separately, and three weeks later the fence is mended and you never learn how.

    This is the drill. Not a sentence — a chair, and staying in it. The uncomfortable part is not that it goes badly. It is that it goes fine without you, and you have to sit there feeling the absence of the job you did not take.

    Ambrose

    Two grown people had a disagreement and settled it, and you were not required. Sit with how that feels, because if it feels like being useless, that is the mask talking and it is worth knowing its voice.

    The road. It is a chair.

    Run it again. Three of these are things people are thanked for.

    All four. The only one that did not cost you an evening is the one where you did nothing — and it is the only one where the fence actually got mended.

    Drill four — you cannot fix it, and it is somebody you love

    Drill three was a mood you could have lifted. This one you cannot, which is much harder.

    Pim has been homesick for about three weeks. She is not in crisis. She works, she eats, she laughs at the right places. She is just quietly, steadily sad, in the way somebody is when the thing they miss is a long way off and not coming back this month.

    There is nothing you can do about it. You have checked.

    What do you do?

    What happened next

    She is busy. She is genuinely grateful. The sad thing goes underground rather than away, and surfaces in November over something trivial — a broken bowl — and neither of you can work out why she is crying about a bowl.

    You did not give her anything to do. You gave her somewhere not to be. And she took it, because being distracted from a thing is easier than sitting in it, and you are the Princess and she is fourteen.

    Ambrose

    Sadness that has been kept busy is not sadness that has finished. You cannot make somebody homesick faster and you should not try to make them homesick less.

    The mask, in an apron.

    What happened next

    YOU. (Tuesday) Alright, Pim?

    PIM. Yes, Majesty.

    YOU. (Wednesday) You alright?

    PIM. Yes, Majesty.

    YOU. (Thursday) Pim —

    PIM. (quickly) I'm alright.

    By Thursday she is reassuring you. The asking has become a small task she performs three times a week, and the correct answer is yes, and she has learned it. You have made her sadness a thing she has to manage on your behalf.

    Ambrose

    Asking once opens a door. Asking every day is standing in the doorway. And the fourth time you ask, you are not asking about her — you are asking her to tell you it is alright, so that you can stop feeling it.

    Not a ditch. Care, repeated until it became a demand.

    What happened next

    YOU. You've been missing home.

    PIM. (a long pause, both paws on the board) Yes.

    YOU. I'm not going to try and fix that. I don't think it can be, and I think you'd rather I didn't try.

    PIM. …Yes.

    YOU. I'm here, though. That's all I've got.

    PIM. (going back to the onions) That's alright.

    Nothing was solved and something was given. You named it, you declined to manage it, and you stayed — and the last of those three is the only one she could not have done for herself.

    She is homesick for another five weeks. She hangs the spoon up handle outward every night of them.

    Ambrose

    Presence is not a lesser thing you offer when you have run out of solutions. It is very often the whole of what was wanted, and it is the one part you were actually able to give.

    The road. Three sentences, and none of them fixed anything.

    What happened next

    She gets through it. People do.

    But she does not know that you noticed, and a fourteen-year-old a long way from home who thinks nobody has noticed draws a conclusion about that — not a loud one, and not one she would ever say out loud, and it goes in with all the others.

    Room is a real gift and this is not it. Room is what you give after you have said you can see her. Before that it is indistinguishable from not looking.

    Ambrose

    The opposite of managing somebody's feelings is not ignoring them. It is witnessing them and then keeping your hands to yourself, which is a great deal harder than either.

    Right ditch — the quiet kind.

    Run it again. The first two are the ones a loving person reaches for.

    All four. Only one of them let her be sad in front of you without needing her to stop. That is the entire skill of this mask and it took three sentences.

    Before you go

    Not a summary, and not a request to be better at conversations. The drills can put a better sentence in your mouth. They cannot give you the thing this mask actually needs, which is the nerve to sit still while somebody you love is unhappy and you are not fixing it.

    Lord, You have never once needed me to hold the room together, and it has held. I have taken on weather that was never mine, and called it love, and been tired in a way I could not explain to anybody. You asked me for my part. You did not ask me for theirs, and You did not make me answerable for how it turns out. Teach me the difference, because I have not been able to find it on my own. Give the growth Yourself, as You always have. And let me be somebody who can sit in a room with an unhappy person and not need them to stop. Amen.

    Now read what is under the Weather-Keeper → — what it protects, why it cannot work, and the two verses that put a limit on what you are answerable for. Better after the ring than before it.

    Practise it

    1. Sit somewhere with somebody who is in a poor mood that has nothing to do with you, and do not improve it. Ten minutes. That is the whole exercise and it is much harder than it sounds.
    2. Say drill four's three sentences out loud. Notice that the middle one — I'm not going to try and fix that — feels like giving up, and is not.
    3. The harder one. Think of somebody whose mood you have been managing for years. If you stopped, what are you afraid would happen? Now ask whether that has ever actually happened.
