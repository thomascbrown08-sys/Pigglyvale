# The Dojo — design rules

A wing of the Pigglyvale site. Named for Carolyn, who loves Japan. The name is an easter egg and can be
changed later without disturbing anything; *The Practice Yard* was the alternative and is used in the prose.

---

## 1. Why it exists

Pigglyvale's premise is that nobody is a villain and everybody is wrong sometimes. That is load-bearing and
must not be broken. But it means **the episodes structurally cannot teach the harder half of this material**
— there is no room in the kingdom for someone operating in bad faith, and putting one there would cost the
site the thing that makes it warm.

The Dojo is where that material lives. It is not a second delivery system for the episodes' lessons; it is
the only place the top of the ladder can be taught at all.

Secondary benefit: it takes weight off the episodes. A difficult character's tactics can be examined here in
five hundred words without an episode having to go dark for two thousand.

## 2. The three hard guards

**This wing could go wrong in a specific way**, and the guards are structural rather than tonal.

1. **Never diagnose a person.** Verbal-self-defence writing reliably teaches readers to see manipulation
   everywhere, including in people who were tired or frightened. Every case is about what to **do** with
   limited information, never about what somebody **is**. *"I can no longer explain this charitably"* is
   honest. *"You are a manipulator"* is a verdict the reader is not entitled to.
2. **Every case ends on the mask on your own face.** Not as an afterthought — it is the most valuable
   section and gets the most care. And it **never ends on the recognition**: it ends with what taking the
   mask off actually sounds like, in sentences the reader could say. Diagnosis without a next sentence is
   just a nicer way of feeling bad.
3. **The Dojo names situations, sentences and masks. It never names the story's machinery.** No harm
   engines, no second-order thinking, no rung ladders. Those stay taught-by-plot in the episodes. This is
   the exact seam where the wing could start explaining the episodes to their own audience, and a future
   session will read "the Dojo explains things" as licence to explain everything. It is not.

## 3. The two axes

**Belts order the cases** by *how much benefit of the doubt the evidence will still bear* — not by how badly
the other person behaved. This builds charity into every rung instead of parking it in belt one, and it
matches how this actually goes wrong in life: not that people cannot take pressure, but that they misread
ordinary friction as malice.

| Belt | What the evidence will bear |
|---|---|
| White | Nothing was aimed. Tired, clumsy, frightened, in a hurry. |
| Yellow | Aimed at the problem, not at you. Ordinary pressure. |
| Green | They are defending themselves badly; their own shame is driving it. |
| Brown | A pattern you can no longer explain charitably. You still do not know their heart. |
| Black | The mask has fused. No repair on offer. Technique runs out. |

**Masks cut across the belts.** The same mask appears at white belt worn for an afternoon and at brown belt
worn for twenty years. Watching one travel up the belts is most of what there is to learn about it.

A **mask is a rationalization** — a sentence a person tells themselves so that what they are doing can go on
feeling alright. It is a state, not an essence; anyone can put one on and they come off. Masks are never
species-coded and never assigned to a permanent outsider class.

> **On the black belt.** A rationalization is a good-faith phenomenon, which appears to empty the top tier.
> It does not. At the top the mask has been worn so long it has fused: the person is no longer telling you a
> story, they are telling themselves one and they believe it. That is why evidence bounces off. This keeps
> the tier genuinely hard while keeping it charitable — and it is the biblical account of the thing, where
> the deceiving is mostly of oneself.

## 4. The shape of a case

Roughly 900–1,400 words. Six parts, in this order.

1. **The rehearsal note** (`.dojo-note`) — one short italic paragraph. Who is wearing the mask, and the fact
   that they are acting. Bruno enjoys it too much; somebody is laughing.
2. **The drill** — the scene in about 120 words, then **the line that lands on you**, set apart.
3. **The mask card** (`.mask`) — four fields, always these four: *what it protects underneath · what it buys
   · why it fails · what Scripture puts in its place.* The Scripture field is not decoration; it does the
   replacing, and the reasoning must be visible rather than asserted.
4. **The roads** (`.road`) — `is-left` caving, `is-right` lashing, `is-path` the road, and usually
   `is-far`, a version further along the same road. Each carries **what it buys** before **what it costs**,
   and the ditches get their goods named sincerely. A reader who caves needs to see why caving is
   understandable, or she will feel caught rather than taught.
5. **Why the last one is best** — and specifically that it is *not the average of the ditches*. Caving and
   lashing are both self-protective. The road costs you something for the other person's sake. If a
   response gets easier as it gets better, it is not the road.
6. **The mask on yourself** (`.onself`) — one narrow question with a real answer, not *do I do this too
   much*. Then what taking it off sounds like, before and after.

Close with **Practise it** (`.talk`) — three exercises, the last one turning it on the reader.

## 5. Register

**Do not score responses as godly and ungodly.** Not because it is untrue, but because of who is reading:
the audience already asks *am I good enough here*, and a page that grades her sentences hands that ache a
new instrument. Say what the road **is** — true, and leaving them a way back — and let the reasoning carry
the theology.

**Depravity is taught silently, by example.** Never named. The Helper case teaches it by showing that the
*good* motive is the problem — that love itself comes out bent, and that there is no motive clean enough to
be safe unexamined. Five of the eight masks are worn by characters the reader likes. That is the argument.

**Scripture does the replacing.** Every mask card ends on a text, and the derivation is shown rather than
cited. The Helper runs on Galatians 6:2 against 6:5 — bear one another's burdens, and each will bear his own
load — where the apparent contradiction resolves in two different words and the resolution *is* the lesson.

## 6. Art

**Mask illustrations are drawn once and reused** across every case that features them. Unlike episode art,
the Dojo's visual cost does not scale with content — which is a real argument for the wing and not only a
convenience. A mask needs one image; a belt needs none.

None have been rolled yet. The specimen case ships text-only and reads fine that way.

## 7. Build order

- **The Helper** — white belt. *Built.* Safest possible entry: it is Thomas, it is canon, the reader has
  already watched him apologise for it, and it establishes the thesis that a mask is love with a fault in it.
- **The Rulebook** — the other-facing case. Miss Quill. Force applied through procedure, which is how most
  people actually get bulldozed.
- **The Joke** — deliberately **not** canon. Nobody in Pigglyvale does this in earnest and nobody should,
  which is exactly why it needs the rehearsal frame. Proves the wing can hold what the kingdom cannot.
- **The Standard** — later, and flagged by the author as personally relevant. The case is discernment about
  *when to speak and when not to*, and why honesty for its own sake is not a virtue on its own. Ephesians
  4:15 is the anchor: the truth is to be spoken **in love**, and the two are one instruction rather than a
  qualification bolted onto a duty.

Build one belt completely before scaffolding the next.
